# Breakdown Categories

Definitions and edge cases for each element category. The question that decides every placement: **which department supplies or arranges this?**

## Cast
Speaking roles (numbered by first appearance) and non-speaking named characters. A character who only appears in a background group is an extra, not cast. Stand-ins, doubles, and voice-only roles (V.O., O.S.) are still cast; note them.

## Extras / Atmosphere
Unnamed background people. Record a count or density ("crowded bar, ~40"). Extras who do something specific on camera (a waiter who serves the lead) are **silent bits**, tagged here with the action noted; they cost more than general atmosphere.

## Stunts
Anything with physical risk beyond normal acting: fights, falls, jumps, car work, fire, drowning, being hit by something. Also note **stunt-adjacent** moments (a slap, a shove) as notes; the stunt coordinator decides if they need cover. Always accompany with a NOTES safety flag.

## Vehicles
Any vehicle seen or used, with motion state: parked/background vs. driven vs. crashed. Picture cars (on camera) differ from background traffic; record make/era if the script states it.

## Props
Handled by cast. If an actor picks it up, uses it, or it is essential to the action, it's a prop. Hero props (plot-critical, seen in close-up, e.g. the letter, the key) deserve a note since they may need duplicates or stunt versions. Weapons are props *and* a safety note.

## Set Dressing
Items that furnish the set but aren't handled: furniture, posters, plants, clutter, signage. If a character interacts with it, it becomes a prop. When the script describes a set in detail ("a cluttered attic of boxes and a dusty mirror"), tag the described items here.

## Wardrobe
Clothing and costume worn by cast, per character. Record as `CHARACTER: item`. Include changes (soaked, torn, bloodied) because they need multiples. Do not invent looks the script doesn't describe.

## Makeup / Hair
Anything beyond standard grooming: wounds, aging, tattoos, wigs, period hair, dirt, sweat, prosthetics. Track state changes across scenes for continuity.

## Special Effects (practical)
On-set physical effects: rain, fog, fire, explosions, squibs/blood hits, breakaway glass, wire work, mechanical rigs. Requires a specialist and usually a safety plan.

## Visual Effects
Added in post: green screen, CGI creatures, set extensions, screen graphics, de-aging. If it can't be done live on set, it's VFX. When a shot could be either, choose by what the script implies and add a note.

## Sound / Music
On-camera sound requirements: a ringing phone, a song playing on a radio that characters hear, a live band. Needs playback or timing on set. Score and post-added effects are not tagged here.

## Animals
Any live animal, with whether it performs or is background. Needs a wrangler and welfare oversight; always flag in NOTES.

## Special Equipment
Gear beyond the normal camera package: crane, Steadicam, underwater housing, drone, helicopter, process trailer, specialist lighting.

## Notes
Everything that affects scheduling, cost, or safety but isn't an item: permits, location restrictions, minors (working-hour limits, chaperones), intimacy coordination, night/weather dependence, dependencies on another scene.

## Page length reference
1 page = 8 eighths. Count lines of the scene against roughly 55 lines per screenplay page. A scene taking a quarter of a page is 2/8; a scene taking 1 page and 3 lines is 1 1/8.
